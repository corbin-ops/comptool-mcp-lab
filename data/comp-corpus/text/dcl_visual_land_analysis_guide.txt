PART 1 — THE SIX VISUAL SIGNALS
What your eyes should read the moment you open any aerial view
Before you look at a single listing, a single price per acre, or a single data point, your eyes need to run a rapid
six-signal scan. These signals are visible from 20,000 feet and they tell you almost everything you need to know
about whether a county is worth deeper investigation.
★ CORE PHILOSOPHY
The aerial view never lies. The market is a reflection of who lives nearby, who drives out on weekends, and
what they can do when they get there. The aerial shows you all three if you know what to look for.

SIGNAL 2 — THE FIELD/GRID PATTERN: The Farmland Flag
One of the most important things to identify instantly is the presence of agricultural grid patterns. These appear
as large, squared-off sections of tan or light green, often arranged in perfectly regular rows and columns. This is
actively farmed land — and it completely changes the market you're operating in.
■ THE FARMLAND PROBLEM
Farmland is a YIELDING ASSET. The people who own it know exactly what it's worth because they're
profiting from it every season. Farmers are not distressed sellers. They understand land value better than
almost anyone. Deep discount acquisition in pure farmland counties is extremely difficult — and even if you
get in, your buyer pool is farmers, not recreational buyers.

Additionally, in counties dominated by farmland, recreational land subdivisions don't work the way you need
them to. Buyers who want to build a cabin or hunt a weekend property aren't shopping in the middle of
cornfields. They want tree cover, privacy, and a sense of remoteness — none of which exists in agricultural
flatland.
Visual fingerprints of farmland counties:
● Perfectly squared parcel shapes — identical sizes, regular grid
● Tan, cream, light green, or off-white color in large contiguous blocks
● Almost no tree canopy — very open, exposed land
● Farm roads running at perfect 90-degree angles
● Grain elevators, large barns, irrigation circles visible on zoom
● ZIP code areas almost completely devoid of wooded sections

SIGNAL 3 — RIDGE STREAKS: The Mountain
Warning System
From altitude, mountain ranges appear as parallel dark green streaks running in the same direction, like
corduroy fabric stretched across the landscape. These are the Appalachian ridge-and-valley formations visible
across Pennsylvania, West Virginia, and neighboring states. The moment you see this pattern, your brain
needs to register: terrain alert — investigate slope and access before anything else.
Why ridge streaks matter:
● Each streak is a forested ridgeline — typically 30% to 70%+ slope
● Steep slopes = no buildable envelope = property that nobody can develop
● Access roads must wind up hollow approaches or switchback ridges — adds cost and limits buyers
● The USABLE acreage of a ridge property may be a fraction of the paper acreage
● Septic systems require perc tests that most steep-slope land will fail
● Buyers researching remotely won't understand the slope until they visit — creates cancellations and
refunds
■ SLOPE DISTORTS ACREAGE VALUE
Never look at a ridge property's acreage at face value. A 20-acre parcel on a mountain ridge may have 3
usable acres. Always verify with the 3D tool and contour lines before pricing or marketing.

SIGNAL 5 — ROAD NETWORK DENSITY: Infrastructure as a Demand
Proxy
Road networks are one of the clearest proxies for human presence and demand. The denser and more varied
the road network, the more people have invested in accessing that land. Sparse, single-route access with long
gaps between roads means isolation — which can be good for seclusion branding but bad for buyer
accessibility and market liquidity.
● Tight, varied road grid — many intersections, roads at multiple angles = suburb or rural-residential, high
infrastructure
● Moderate road grid — roads every mile or two, some variation = rural residential, ideal zone
● Single corridor roads — one main road with long spurs = very remote, access is the product's limitation
● No visible roads in large areas — state forest, wilderness, or extreme terrain = avoid
SIGNAL 6 — POPULATION ANCHORS: The Buyer Pool Indicator
Your recreational land buyer is almost never a local. They're someone driving 45 to 90 minutes from a
population center — a city or large town — to reach their weekend escape. This means every county you
evaluate needs a credible population anchor within a reasonable driving radius. If there's no anchor, there's no
buyer pool.
✓ THE POPULATION ANCHOR RULE
The 60-90 minute rule: your property needs to sit within 60-90 minutes of a city or metro area large enough
to generate recreational land buyers. Counties that are isolated from any real population center will have
extreme sell times regardless of how pretty the land is.
● Look for town names with visible road hubs and density
● Interstate highway presence = connectivity to metro areas
● Airport symbols, regional hospitals visible = real population
● County seats with actual grids of streets = anchor towns
● The further from any of these, the thinner your buyer pool

 TIER 1
 TOO RURAL — SKIP
This is the wilderness end of the spectrum. Counties at Tier 1 are dominated by state forests, national forests,
or game lands. Tree canopy is unbroken from border to border. There are almost no listings because there's
almost no private land being actively marketed. The few listings that exist show extreme price variance because
comping is nearly impossible — every parcel is so unique that market value is essentially undefined.
Aerial characteristics:
● Solid, uniform dark green from edge to edge — no visible breaks in canopy
● Ridge streak patterns if mountainous terrain
● State/National Forest labels cover large portions of the county
● Very few road corridors — mostly single-lane access roads into deep hollows
● Almost zero listing density on the map — maybe 2-5 per county at most
● The listings that exist have enormous price range ($1k-$30k ppa) with no clear pattern
Pennsylvania Tier 1 examples: Cameron, Potter, Forest, deep Clinton County
Cameron County is the extreme case — Emporium is the only real town and it barely registers. The entire
county is essentially Sproul State Forest and Elk State Forest. There is no recreational buyer pool that can
sustain a land business. Even hunters, who are often the last remaining buyer type in counties like this, can
typically hunt for free on adjacent public land.

 TIER 2
 VERY RURAL — APPROACH WITH CAUTION
Tier 2 counties have private land but it's deeply forested with minimal population. There are towns, but they're
small — typically under 5,000 people — and isolated. The buyer pool exists but it's thin and seasonal.
Properties move slowly. Comping is hard because comparable sales are infrequent. You can operate here but
you need to be very precise about which specific parcels within the county you target.
Pennsylvania Tier 2 examples: Venango, northern Clearfield, northern Jefferson, McKean
Michigan Tier 2 equivalent: Au Sable State Forest units in Roscommon's NE corner
Key test for Tier 2:
● Is there a town of 5,000+ within 30 miles? If no, it's probably Tier 1
● Are there at least 10-15 active listings visible in the county? If fewer, market is too thin
● Is there any highway infrastructure connecting to a larger metro? If not, buyer pool is regional only
● Are the listings priced consistently or randomly? Wild variance = bad comping environment
 TIER 3
 MODERATE RURAL — SELECTIVE YES
Tier 3 is where you start making real money if you're selective. These counties are genuinely rural — real tree
cover, real privacy feel, recreational activities available — but they have enough population and infrastructure
that a functioning land market exists. You can find comps. Properties move within a reasonable timeframe. The
buyer pool extends to people driving from regional cities.
Pennsylvania Tier 3 examples: Southern Clearfield, southern Jefferson, Clarion, parts of Crawford
The key distinction between Tier 2 and Tier 3 is whether you can find 5 defensible comps in a reasonable
acreage range. If you can, you're in Tier 3 territory. If every comp you pull is 10+ miles away and 5 years old,
you're in Tier 2.

 TIER 4
 THE SWEET SPOT — YOUR TARGET MARKET
Tier 4 is the zone where your entire business model works optimally. These counties have the recreational feel
buyers want — real woods, real privacy, accessible without being suburban — AND they sit within comfortable
driving distance of a metro area large enough to generate consistent buyer demand. Properties move. Comps
exist. Price discovery is real. You can buy at 50 cents on the dollar, subdivide or flip, and find buyers within your
target timeline.
✓ THE THREE-REQUIREMENT RULE
Tier 4 is the intersection of three requirements: (1) rural enough to feel like an escape, (2) close enough to a
population center that buyers exist, and (3) developed enough that a functioning real estate market with real
comps exists. All three must be true simultaneously.
Pennsylvania Tier 4 examples: Mercer, Butler, Armstrong, Indiana, Cambria, parts of Lawrence
Ohio Tier 4 examples: Columbiana, Portage, parts of Trumbull
Michigan Tier 4 examples: Areas near recreational lakes — Roscommon lake corridor
Why these counties work:
● Pittsburgh metro (2.3M+ population) creates enormous demand for weekend properties in PA and OH
● Cleveland-Akron metro feeds Portage, Stark, Trumbull, Mahoning
● Detroit and Flint metro feed Roscommon and surrounding MI counties
● Properties can be listed and sold remotely — buyers research online and visit once
● Recreational activities (hunting, ATV, camping, fishing) are all accessible
● No-restriction parcels allow buyers to put what they want on the land
 TIER 5
 BORDERLINE URBAN — COMPLICATED
Tier 5 counties have significant urban or suburban sections but also retain rural areas. The challenge is that
you're operating in a mixed market — part of the county has land prices inflated by suburban demand, another
part has recreational buyer potential, and a third part is just developed enough that 'rural land' listings look out
of place. You need to be county-section specific rather than treating the whole county as a single market.
Examples: Westmoreland PA, Washington PA, Trumbull OH, Stark OH
The specific risk in Tier 5: you can easily mistake suburban sprawl for rural land potential from altitude.
Washington County PA looks manageable from 30,000 feet but zoomed in it's riddled with small townships
packed densely together — there's continuous low-density suburban development all the way down from
Pittsburgh. The township-level zoning complexity in PA makes this even harder to navigate.
 TIER 6
 TOO URBAN — SKIP ENTIRELY
Tier 6 counties are simply not a land investing market. Allegheny County PA (Pittsburgh) is the clearest
example — it is continuous urban and suburban development from edge to edge. There is no recreational land
to buy. Even if a vacant parcel exists, it's priced at development land values, not recreational values, and the

PART 3 — TERRAIN & TOPOGRAPHY
The 3D Verification System and Reading the Land's Physical Story
Color and tier classification get you to the right county. Terrain analysis gets you to the right parcel. This section
covers everything you need to know about reading physical landscape features that directly affect buildability,
access, value, and marketability.
THE MANDATORY 3D VERIFICATION STEP
★ RULE: 3D ON EVERY SINGLE PROPERTY
Run the 3D terrain view on EVERY property before you do anything else with it. No exceptions. It takes 30
seconds. It has caught deal-killers that the flat aerial completely hid. If the terrain jumps off the screen
dramatically in 3D, you have a slope problem. If it's flat or gently rolling, you're clear to proceed.
The 3D tilt function in Land Insights (and in Google Maps/Earth) allows you to rotate the view to see elevation.
By right-clicking and adjusting the perspective, you convert the flat satellite view into a rendered topographic
display that makes ridges, valleys, and slope severity immediately obvious.
What the 3D view reveals that flat aerial hides:
● True ridge height and steepness — a 'green area' on flat aerial could be a 60-degree slope in 3D
● Valley floor location — where the buildable, flat land actually sits
● Hollow approach routes — how access roads must navigate around terrain
● River and creek flood plain proximity — low areas that look flat but flood
● Power line cuts across ridges — these appear as diagonal scars in 3D
● State forest/game land adjacency — how much of the terrain is permanently public
READING CONTOUR LINES
When you enable the topographic layer on Land Insights or switch to terrain view, you'll see contour lines —
curved lines that follow equal-elevation paths across the land. These lines are the single most important tool for
assessing slope on a specific parcel.

POWER LINES & UTILITY EASEMENTS
Power lines are one of the most damaging value factors you can encounter on a parcel. From the aerial or 3D
view, they appear as a straight, cleared diagonal or straight cut running through the property — often orange or
brown in color in 3D view, sometimes appearing as a bare slash through otherwise dense canopy from above.
■ POWER LINE = SIGNIFICANT VALUE REDUCTION
A power line easement through a parcel means: (1) you cannot build under or near it — setbacks are
typically hundreds of feet from high-voltage lines, (2) the easement breaks up the usable area and kills
privacy, (3) high-voltage lines produce audible buzzing that buyers hate, and (4) the cleared easement is
permanently visible and disrupts the rural-privacy feel your buyers are paying for. Always flag it immediately.
● High-voltage transmission lines (giant metal towers) = most damaging, avoid
● Distribution lines on wooden poles = less severe, still note it
● Gas pipeline easements = cleared strips, can't build, flag and discount
● Look for: straight diagonal cuts through canopy from above, orange/brown scars in 3D
● Verify easement width — some are 300+ feet wide, consuming massive usable area

MICHIGAN: ROSCOMMON COUNTY
Roscommon County is an excellent teaching example because it contains nearly every zone type within a
single county boundary — lake-premium corridor, moderate rural residential areas, state forest buffer zones,
and genuinely remote southern sections. Reading this county correctly requires understanding where within the
county a property sits, not just what county it's in.
Roscommon County, Michigan — Full county aerial with active listings. Note the heavy listing concentration around Houghton Lake
 (west) and Roscommon town (north), thinning dramatically toward the south and Au Sable Forest (NE).
Zone-by-Zone Breakdown:
Zone 1 — Houghton Lake Area (West/Center)
The hottest cluster in the county. Listings in the 7.35k to 8.14k ppa range right around the lake. This premium is
entirely lake-proximity driven — buyers pay for recreational access to Michigan's largest inland lake. The
20.00ac at 3.45k ppa just west of the lake tells you that once you step even a mile away from the lake pull,
prices drop dramatically. The gradient is steep and fast.
Zone 2 — Roscommon Town Area (North)

Zone 3 — Saint Helen (East)
The 12.24ac at 12.75k ppa is the highest on the full county map — nearly certainly waterfront or near-waterfront

★ ZONE DETERMINES MARKET
Key Roscommon insight: You are not buying 'a Roscommon County property.' You are buying a
lake-adjacent property, a town-adjacent property, or a remote property. Each has a completely different
market, buyer pool, and hold time. Always identify the zone first.

PENNSYLVANIA: THE RIDGE-AND-VALLEY SYSTEM
The Appalachian ridge-and-valley terrain is the defining geographic feature of central Pennsylvania and the
single most important terrain pattern to recognize. Counties like Mifflin, Juniata, Perry, Huntingdon, and Centre
all feature this formation. Understanding it is non-negotiable.
Centre / Mifflin / Juniata / Perry / Snyder / Huntingdon counties PA. The ridge-and-valley Appalachian signature is crystal clear from
altitude: parallel dark green ridges alternating with lighter valley floors. State College visible (top left) provides population anchor for
 Centre County.
From this altitude the ridges look like fabric — parallel dark stripes running diagonally across the frame. Each
dark stripe is a forested ridge at significant elevation with steep slopes on both faces. The lighter areas between
them are the valley floors where towns, farms, and development actually exist. Any parcel sitting ON the ridge
(dark zone) is in the terrain-risk category. Parcels in the valley floors are a different conversation entirely.

WASHINGTON COUNTY PA — The Deceptive Suburban County

■ PENNSYLVANIA TOWNSHIP COMPLEXITY
Pennsylvania has hundreds of independent townships each with their own zoning. A parcel that looks
promising on the aerial may be in a township with 1-acre minimum lots and strict septic setbacks while the
adjacent township allows 10+ acre natural land. Always verify at the township level, not just the county level.

PART 5 — THE COMPLETE EVALUATION
WORKFLOW
Step-by-step: from first aerial view to go/no-go decision
Every county and every property evaluation should follow this sequence. Steps are ordered by speed — fastest
eliminations come first so you don't waste time analyzing something you should have killed in 30 seconds.
STEP 1 — OPEN AERIAL & RUN THE SIX-SIGNAL SCAN (60 seconds)
Open the satellite view at full-county zoom. Read color, texture, field patterns, ridge streaks, road density, and
population anchors simultaneously. This takes 60 seconds and eliminates 40% of counties before you do
anything else.
● Is the dominant color uniform dark green? → Check tier — possible Tier 1-2
● Is there a clear agricultural grid pattern? → Flag farmland — likely skip
● Are there ridge streaks (Appalachian corduroy pattern)? → Terrain flag
● Is there visible road infrastructure and town presence? → Good sign
● Can I identify at least one real population anchor? → Confirm buyer pool exists
STEP 2 — TIER CLASSIFICATION (30 seconds)
Based on the six-signal scan, assign a tier (1-6). If you're assigning Tier 1, 2, or 6, stop here. Document the
reason and move on. Only continue for Tiers 3, 4, and 5.
● Tier 1 or 2 → Stop. Document: 'Too rural — thin market, extreme comping difficulty'
● Tier 6 → Stop. Document: 'Too urban — no recreational land market'
● Tier 3 → Proceed with caution, expect selective parcel requirements
● Tier 4 → Proceed — this is your zone
● Tier 5 → Proceed with caution, expect township/location complexity
STEP 3 — 3D TERRAIN VERIFICATION (30-60 seconds)
Right-click and tilt the view to 3D for any Tier 3-5 county or any specific parcel you're evaluating. This is
mandatory and non-negotiable. A Tier 4 county can still have Tier 1 parcels on steep ridges within its borders.
● Does the terrain jump dramatically in 3D? → Slope flag — check contour lines
● Is the parcel in a valley floor? → Good — likely buildable
● Is the parcel on a ridge face? → Check contour line density immediately
● Are contour lines packed tight? → Slope too steep — discount or eliminate
● Any diagonal scars or straight-line cuts? → Power line easement — flag it

[PAGE 34]
DewClaw Visual Land Analysis Guide | Page 34
STEP 4 — PUBLIC LAND AUDIT (30 seconds)
Check for state forest, national forest, game lands, wildlife areas within or adjacent to the subject parcel. Use
the land layer overlay.
● Is the parcel itself within public land boundaries? → Not private — eliminate
● Is parcel surrounded on multiple sides by public land? → Extreme isolation risk
● Is public land adjacent on one side? → Seclusion premium possible — note it
● Are there significant game lands fragmeting the local market? → Thin buyer pool warning
STEP 5 — LISTING DENSITY & PRICE CHECK (2-3 minutes)
Now look at the listings visible on the map. This is your first market data read.
● Count visible listings — fewer than 5-6 in county = thin market warning
● Read the price per acre range — is it consistent or wildly variable?
● Wild PPA variance (e.g., 0.75k to 57k in same county) = bad comping environment
● Consistent PPA range with normal acreage variation = functioning market
● Identify any obvious premium zones (lake-adjacent, highway-adjacent) vs discount zones
STEP 6 — LOCATION ZONE IDENTIFICATION
Identify which zone within the county the subject parcel sits in. County tier classification is a starting point —
zone within the county is the final answer.
● Lake-adjacent zone → Premium pricing, fast market, higher entry cost
● Town-adjacent zone → Mid-tier pricing, consistent demand
● Highway corridor zone → Good access, commuter-adjacent premium possible
● Interior rural zone → Recreational pricing, longer hold times
● State forest buffer zone → Seclusion premium possible, thin market
● Agricultural zone within otherwise rural county → Farmland dynamics apply

MARKET VELOCITY IS SEASONAL — LAND VALUE IS NOT
Land values are stable year-round — do not time purchases around season. What changes is market
velocity: winter in northern states (MI, OH, PA) slows sell times 20-40% because buyers cannot see, access,
or visualize the property. Buy in winter if you get the deal. Market in spring/summer with fresh drone content.

CLOSING NOTES
The philosophy behind all of this
Every principle in this guide comes back to a single core insight: the aerial view is a reflection of human
behavior. Where people live, how they use land, who drives out on weekends, and what they're willing to pay —
all of it is encoded in what the satellite sees from above.
Farmland looks the way it does because farmers are actively working it. Mountain ridges look uninhabited
because they are — you can't build there and no one wants to try. Lake-adjacent land commands a premium
because there's a massive line of people who want to be near water. Urban density looks congested because it
is — every square foot has been claimed.
Your job is to find the counties and parcels where a recreational buyer's desire to escape meets the real-world
possibility of getting there and doing something meaningful with the land. The aerial tells you, in 90 seconds,
whether that intersection exists.
★ THE FINAL RULE
Read the aerial. Trust what it's telling you. Verify with 3D. Check the listings for market confirmation. Then
act decisively. The system works when you follow it without skipping steps — and skips almost nothing when
you've trained your eye to read the signals correctly.
