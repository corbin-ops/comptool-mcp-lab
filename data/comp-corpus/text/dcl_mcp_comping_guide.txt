I. CORE PHILOSOPHY & BUSINESS MODEL
DewClaw buys vacant rural land at approximately 50% of market value, then sells slightly below market using
elite drone marketing. Target hold time: 7–90 days. The entire business model depends on one thing: knowing
what a property is actually worth so we can offer 50 cents on the dollar with confidence.
Why Comping Matters
The comp process is not about finding a number to justify a predetermined offer. It's about understanding what
the market has actually decided land in this county, of this type, of this acreage, is worth. Everything else — the
offer, the negotiation, the value-add play — flows from that honest number.
The Value-Add Backup Strategy
If a seller rejects the 50% as-is offer, look for value-add potential (subdivision, rezoning, etc.). If the improved
value is $160K and the seller wants $80K, that's still 50% of the improved value — and the seller doesn't know
the upside exists. Always identify value-adds even when you don't need them for the primary offer. They are the
backup plan.

III. URL & NAVIGATION FUNDAMENTALS
The Only URL Format We Use
Every property will come in as a data?parcel= URL. This is the standard format:
https://app.landinsights.co/data?parcel=[PARCEL_ID]#[ZOOM]/[LAT]/[LNG]
If the MCP browser cannot load the parcel page, immediately tell the user to input the data parcel manually.
Never try to force it or use the comp report URL as a fallback.
Modals & Page Interruptions
Land Insights occasionally displays update modals like 'We've made some improvements.' When this happens:
click OK immediately and move on. The page will not lose its state. Even if it refreshes, all previously read data
remains in working memory. Simply continue clicking the remaining tabs. You already know everything you
collected before the refresh.
County Boundary Lines
Pink lines on the Land Insights map are county boundaries. They are your hard comp border. Never pull a
comp from across a pink line, even if it's physically closer. Buyers search Zillow/Redfin by county — adjacent
counties develop completely different pricing patterns over time.

V. THE FULL STEP-BY-STEP COMPING WORKFLOW
★ TARGET: Complete every property evaluation in approximately 2.5 minutes.
STEP 1 — Open the Land Insights Parcel Page
Navigate the MCP browser to the data?parcel= URL. Confirm the page loads and the parcel data is visible. If a
modal appears, click OK. If login is required, notify the user immediately.
STEP 2 — Pull Summary Data from Land Insights
In order: Summary tab (last purchase price, acreage, APN), Parcel tab (land use classification), Owner tab
(out-of-state/county status, ownership length). Note Tax Delinquent status, Family Transfer flag, Mobile Home
flag, Structure count. Do this fast — do not linger.
STEP 3 — Open Land ID Simultaneously
Open a new tab to Land ID. Click AI Smart Search (blue button) → Parcel → ID → enter State / County / APN.
Copy the URL and paste it in Extra Notes as 'Land ID link.' If APN doesn't aggregate, try Owner Name. If Land
ID is blocked entirely, note it and skip.
STEP 4 — Street View (MANDATORY — No Exceptions)
Drop the pegman onto Street View for every subject property. Look for: driveways/entryways, cleared building
spots, mobile homes, sheds, any structures, wet areas/standing water, power lines, fencing, road condition.
Note everything observed in Extra Notes.
STEP 5 — Satellite Verification
Check satellite imagery on both Google Maps AND Land Insights with ALL filters OFF. Toggle between old and
new satellite imagery to catch recent timbering. Never trust filters alone — use your own eyes.
STEP 6 — Check MLS Comps Toggle
MLS Comps should always be ON. If zero comps are showing (no actives, no solds, no pendings): go to Data
Layers (bottom right, next to Tools and Map Layers) → click MLS Data → toggle ON. Only do this if there are
genuinely zero comps. If even a few are showing, it's already on.
STEP 7 — Navigate to County View for Comps
Navigate directly to zoom level 11-12 in the URL for county-wide comp view. Do NOT scroll-zoom 4-5 times —
type the zoom level into the URL directly to save time. Acreage Range: always click AUTO. If not enough
comps: set below=80%, above=150%.
STEP 8 — Click Each Comp Bubble
Click each comp bubble to open the popup (acreage, price, PPA, status, DOM, thumbnail). Click the green
Details button. Review photos, MLS price, days on market. Then click 'View Original Listing' in the bottom right
to open Zillow or Redfin.

[PAGE 8]
STEP 9 — Verify the Comp on Zillow and Redfin
Always click both if both are available. Check: Is it MLS or off-market/public record? Read all photos. Read the
description. Get the APN (may be under 'Show more' or 'Show all property details'). Check price history for
drops or cycles. Check DOM and cumulative DOM.
STEP 10 — Pull APN into Land Insights
Duplicate the Land Insights tab. Input the comp's APN. Verify the parcel loads correctly. Compare its deeded
vs. calculated acreage. Click 5-7 surrounding parcels if anything looks off. This turns a questionable comp into
a fully verified one.
STEP 11 — Visual Map Cross-Check
On Zillow/Redfin, expand the neighborhood map. Toggle to Satellite + Lot Lines. Confirm the comp is in the
right general vicinity. If parcel lines show, compare to listing photos. If APN didn't aggregate, try clicking
surrounding parcels on Land Insights to find it visually.
STEP 12 — Final Valuation & Deliverable
Find your anchor comp, floor, and ceiling. Weight by: proximity to subject acreage, geographic closeness, land
type similarity, recency of sale. Calculate market value. Generate offer range (target ~50% of MV). Deliver
complete 8-section analysis in ONE response.
STEP 13 — Clean Up
CLOSE ALL MCP TABS after every comp. Delete the tab group completely. The browser will lag and crash if
you leave them open. No exceptions.

IX. APN — THE MOST POWERFUL TOOL IN COMPING
★ The APN is not just a verification tool. It is a full comp on the comp itself. When you pull a comp's
APN into Land Insights, you see everything about it — exactly as if you were comping it as a subject
property.
When you input a comp's APN into Land Insights, you gain access to: flood zones, wetlands, road access,
parcel shape, calculated vs. deeded acreage, land use classification, area quality, surrounding parcels, and
satellite/Street View. This transforms a questionable comp into a fully verified, deeply understood one.
Where to Find the APN
 Zillow: Scroll to Facts & Features → Property section → 'Parcel number' (may need 'Show more')
 
 Redfin: Scroll to Property Details → click 'Show all property details' → Lot Information → 'Parcel Number'
 
 Land Insights sidebar: APN field visible on the data?parcel= page left sidebar
 
 Note: Zillow's 'Parcel number' and Land Insights' 'APN' may show different numbers — verify both
 
How to Use the APN
Duplicate the Land Insights browser tab. In the duplicated tab, navigate to the data?parcel= URL and input the
comp's APN in the parcel search. If it loads — you now have a full Land Insights view of the comp property.
Verify everything: acreage, flood zone, wetlands, access, parcel shape, land type. This is a comp on steroids.
When APN Doesn't Aggregate
Sometimes the APN simply doesn't work in Land Insights. This happens — don't panic. Close that tab and
continue working the comp with available information. When running multiple comps simultaneously, if one tab's
APN fails, close it and move to the next.
The Visual Parcel Hunt (Advanced Technique)
If APN fails but you know the approximate location from the Zillow/Redfin map, try clicking surrounding parcels
on Land Insights to find it visually. If you find a neighboring parcel that matches the acreage and shape from the
listing photos, click it — you've found the comp parcel without needing the APN at all. You already have it right
there. Skip the tab duplication entirely.
■ If the comp bubble's position on Land Insights looks far off from expected location BUT the APN
confirms the correct location — ALWAYS go with the APN. The bubble can be wrong. The APN does not
lie.

X. ACREAGE VERIFICATION PROTOCOL
Acreage discrepancies between deeded (reported) and calculated acres are common. Some difference is
normal. A 3-4+ acre gap is a red flag that requires investigation.
The Surrounding Parcel Method
Click 5-7 neighboring parcels on Land Insights and compare their deeded AND calculated acreage. Build a
mental picture of the size relationships. If your subject says 10 acres but neighbors are all 20-40 acres and the
subject looks half their size, the deeded acreage is likely wrong. If calculated acreage aligns with the neighbors'
scale, calculated is probably correct.
The House Reference Trick
A house typically sits on approximately 1 acre. If a property has a house on it and the deeded acreage says it's
10 acres, but the whole parcel looks the same size as a house lot, something is off. Use visible structures as a
proportional reference anchor.

XIII. READING LISTING PHOTOS & DESCRIPTIONS
Listing photos and descriptions are used to cross-reference and verify what the map and satellite show. They
are not taken at face value — they are one piece of the puzzle.
Photo Reading Protocol
 Go through ALL photos on Zillow and Redfin — don't just look at the first one
 
 Note what land type the photos show: pasture, wooded, cleared, farmland, etc.
 
 Look for any structures visible in photos — shed, trailer, barn, mobile home, anything
 
 Look for water features — pond, creek, wetland markers
 
 Look for road frontage shots — can you tell if it's paved or dirt?
 
 Compare what the photos show to what the satellite/map shows — do they match?
 
 If photos and satellite don't match, investigate why — wrong pin? Wrong listing photos?
 
Description Reading Protocol
 Read the full description — don't skim
 
 Look for: road type mentioned, utility availability, building restrictions, deed restrictions
 
 Flag any mention of easements, setbacks, HOA, or deed restrictions
 
 Note phrases like 'multiple tracts available' — signals subdivision-style sale
 
 Note phrases like 'gently rolling terrain' vs 'heavily wooded' vs 'flat and cleared'
 
 Cross-reference description with photos — do they tell the same story?
 
 If description mentions something specific (creek, pond, spring), find it on satellite
 
When Photos Are Missing or Terrible Quality
If photos are missing or extremely low quality, and the description is vague, and APN didn't aggregate — this
comp drops to Tier 3 (outlier). You can still use it if it fits the trend line, but never anchor your valuation on it.
Flag it explicitly. More information always means more confidence.

XIV. STREET VIEW PROTOCOL
■ Street View is MANDATORY for every subject property. No exceptions. Ever.
Street View is the only way to see the world at ground level — the thing satellite cannot give you. It shows road
quality, neighborhood condition, house quality, power line locations, driveway access, standing water, fencing,
and general area feel.
What to Look For in Street View
 Road condition: Paved vs. dirt/gravel. Cracked old asphalt is still paved. Dirt roads reduce value and
 accessibility.
 Power/utility poles: Running alongside the road means electricity is accessible. Positive for buildability.
 
 High-voltage transmission lines: Massive towers with thick cables — require wide setbacks, significantly
 reduce value.
 Driveway/entry access: Is there a clear driveway or access point to the property? Gravel, paved, or none?
 
 Houses nearby: What condition are they in? Well-maintained vs. collapsing? This tells you neighborhood
 quality.
 Standing water or wet areas: Look in ditches, low spots, fields — indicates potential flooding or drainage
 issues.
 Fencing: Is the property fenced? What type? Barbed wire, wood, chain-link?
 
 Structures: Any visible buildings, barns, sheds, mobile homes on or adjacent to subject parcel?
 
 Overgrowth or abandonment: Heavily overgrown roadsides with collapsed structures = declining area.
 
The Utility Easement Street View Hack
If you see a cleared strip on satellite that might be a utility easement, and you can't drop your pegman directly
on it (no road coverage there), follow that easement line on the map until it crosses a road — even if that's 2
miles away. Drop your pegman there. The easement will look the same at that road crossing as it does at the
subject property. This lets you identify: power lines vs. telecom poles vs. pipeline vs. other infrastructure.

XVI. STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION & FLAGGING
✓ ANY structure on a property — shed, trailer, mobile home, barn, house — must be flagged. Even the
smallest structure gets noted. The seller conversation handles condition assessment.
I cannot assess condition or value with certainty from satellite and Street View alone. My job is to identify that
something is there and describe what I can observe. The user then asks the seller directly about condition, age,
and situation.
Structure Flag Format
When flagging a structure, provide: what it appears to be, where on the parcel it sits, what source revealed it
(satellite, Street View, listing photos), and any observable condition notes. Example: 'Structure flagged:
Appears to be a metal barn/outbuilding, visible in satellite view on the southeast corner of the parcel. Listing
photos show interior — appears to be a working agricultural structure, no obvious collapse or severe damage
visible.'
How Structures Affect Comps
A comp with a structure is still usable. Note the structure in your comp analysis. If your subject property is
vacant land and the comp has a house on it, the comp's price likely reflects the structure's value — weight it
accordingly. A comp with a small shed or outbuilding is generally still comparable to vacant land.

 Multiple price drops over time = seller originally overpriced it, market corrected it
 
 Listed → Delisted → Relisted multiple times = high red flag, seller extremely reluctant

 Price raised at any point = unusual — flag and investigate
 
 Original list price vs. final sale price gap tells you how much the market pushed back

XVIII. LAND TYPE IDENTIFICATION
Farmland
Actively farmed — visible crop rows, irrigation patterns, tilled soil, distinct field boundaries. This is a YIELDING
ASSET. It produces income. Worth significantly MORE than other land types. Always identify and call out
farmland. If you see crop rows from satellite, that is farmland.
Pasture / Cleared
Open grass, possibly grazed. No crop rows. May have livestock visible. Clean, usable, buildable. Generally
worth more than wooded because it requires less clearing work. Buyers can visualize building a home on it
immediately.
Wooded / Timber
Tree canopy covering the parcel. Value depends on timber quality, species, age, and density. Pine plantation
(uniform rows visible from satellite) has timber value. Mixed hardwood varies. Wooded land is generally worth
less per acre than cleared because of clearing costs.
Recently Timbered / Clear-Cut
Stumps, debris, ugly brown/gray patches on satellite. Worst of both worlds — not wooded (no
privacy/recreation), not cleared (stumps everywhere, can't build easily). Discount 5-30% depending on severity.
Toggle old vs. new satellite to catch it.
Wetlands / Flood Zone
Blue overlays on Land Insights hazards layer, standing water visible on satellite, irregular dark patches
indicating saturated soil. Significantly reduces buildable area. Never panic-discount without checking the actual
percentage. 100% flood zone is very different from 10% flood zone on a corner.
