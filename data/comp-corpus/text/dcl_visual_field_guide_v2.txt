Multiple thick bundled cables running in parallel. Enormous cleared corridor underneath — 295-400ft wide. Nothing
 built anywhere near it. Standard wooden utility pole visible at far right for scale comparison — it looks tiny.
[HIGH-VOLTAGE] Same Corridor — Different Angle

[PAGE 4]
Fig 1B: Same easement corridor from a different Street View angle. The wide cleared swath through the landscape
is unmistakable. Wooded areas on both sides, nothing in the corridor. The tower in the background confirms this is
 the same high-voltage line. Standard distribution poles visible on the right — note the dramatic size difference.
High-Voltage Easement on Land Insights Map
[LAND INSIGHTS MAP] High-Voltage Easement Corridor — Bird's Eye
 Fig 1C: Land Insights satellite view showing a massive high-voltage transmission easement cutting diagonally
 across the entire map as a wide pink/red corridor. Width labels visible: 295-ft, 328-ft, 361-ft. This cuts through

 Insights, it is transmission-level. Always flag immediately.
Reference: Power Line Scale Comparison
[REFERENCE] Voltage vs. Tower Size Scale
 Fig 1D: Voltage vs. tower size reference chart. Left to right: 44-69kV wooden H-frame (smallest, distribution level),
 44-161kV small steel lattice, 120-315kV medium lattice, 230-315kV monopole, 450-735kV massive X-frame lattice
 (largest). Size directly correlates with voltage and discount severity.

[PAGE 5]
[REFERENCE] Transmission vs. Distribution — Size Comparison
 Fig 1E: Transmission vs. Distribution comparison chart. LEFT side (Transmission): Steel lattice tower 120ft+.
RIGHT side (Distribution): Wooden poles 37-85ft. The distribution poles on the right are what you see running
 alongside roads — they are NOT transmission lines. Never confuse them.

The Street View Easement Detective Technique
When you see a cleared strip on satellite but can't drop Street View directly on it (no road coverage), follow that
easement line on the map until it crosses a road — even 2 miles away. Drop your pegman there. The easement
will look exactly the same at that road crossing. You'll be able to visually identify what it is immediately. Once
identified, pull the APN to confirm the full impact on the subject parcel.

IDENTIFICATION

Recently Timbered — Bare/Dirt Stage (Worst)
[RECENTLY TIMBERED] Bare Earth Stage — Pink/Sandy Exposed Soil Visible
 Fig 4A: Zoomed Land Insights view showing a recently timbered parcel. The pink/sandy exposed soil color

area too. Toggle map layers here is critical because one layer may show bare earth and another may still show old
 tree canopy imagery.

 5-30%.
[REGROWTH] Zoomed In — Lighter Interior vs. Dark Mature Surrounding Canopy

surrounding dark mature canopy is clearly visible. This is what you're looking for — a parcel that looks lighter,

What Mature Timber Looks Like at Ground Level
The top photo in Fig 5B is what you're looking for in listing photos to confirm high timber value. Key visual
markers:
 Tall straight trunks — 60-80ft+ with clean lower trunks (no low branches)
 
 High canopy — the green is way above your head, not scrubby and low
 
 Clean forest floor — mature trees shade out undergrowth, floor is relatively clear
 
 Good sight lines through the forest — you can see a long distance between trunks
 
 Dark bark — mature hardwoods (oak, maple, hickory) have dark, furrowed bark
 
 Uniform height — all trees roughly same age/height indicates managed or old-growth stand

VII. LAND INSIGHTS — SUMMARY TAB DEEP DIVE
The Summary tab is the first tab and the one that loads by default. Every field lives in a specific location. Here is
exactly what to read and in what order.
Owner Name + Acreage + APN
Top of sidebar. Owner name bold. Acreage and APN on same line. '11 properties' link shows how many
properties this owner holds — useful context.
Orange Tags (Structure / Corp Owned)
Appear directly below the owner name line. CATCH THESE FIRST. Structure = something exists on the parcel.
Corp Owned = LLC or corporate ownership (sophisticated seller, knows value).
Comp Report + KML buttons
Ignore Comp Report — never use it. KML is for downloading parcel data.
AI Comp value
NEVER use this. It appears next to KML. Always ignore it completely.
Summary / Parcel / Owner / Insights / Metrics tabs
Click through all three main tabs: Summary, Parcel, Owner.
APN field
Critical — this is what you duplicate tab and use to pull comp into Land Insights.
GPS Coordinates
Use if APN fails — can drop into Google Maps to confirm physical location.

VIII. HAZARDS LAYER — ON vs. OFF PROTOCOL
■ ALWAYS toggle hazards back ON after turning them off to assess terrain. Never leave the property
assessment with hazards toggled off.
Hazards ON — What the Map Looks Like
[HAZARDS ON] Blue Overlays Active — Wetlands, Surface Water, Flood Zones Highlighted
 Fig 8A: Hazards layer ON — the same Wexford County area. Massive blue overlays dominate the landscape. Healy
 Lake is obvious. Wetlands and surface water highlighted throughout. The subject parcel (cyan outline) sits near
significant blue coverage. This tells me: water is a major feature of this landscape and I need to assess carefully.
Hazards OFF — Real Satellite Visible
[HAZARDS OFF] Real Satellite Visible — Timber Organization Readable, True Terrain Visible

[PAGE 25]
Fig 8B: Hazards layer OFF — same area. Now I can see real satellite imagery. Healy Lake still visible as dark water
 body but overlays gone. The wooded parcels are clearly readable — and notably this area shows organized
plantation timber canopy. The small utility easement line visible running above Highway 115 across the river. This
 is where actual terrain assessment happens.
The Exact Toggle Protocol
 Step 1: Open Data Layers panel (bottom right button)
 
 Step 2: Find 'All Hazards' at the top of the panel
 
 Step 3: Toggle OFF to assess real terrain — blue overlays disappear
 
 Step 4: Do full visual assessment: timber, structures, easements, road access
 
 Step 5: Toggle ALL HAZARDS back ON when done
 
 Step 6: Confirm hazards are back on before moving to comps

The 10-Parcel Click Rule
 Click up to 10 surrounding parcels maximum — should take 15-20 seconds total
 
 Check acreage in sidebar against comp bubble acreage for each click
 
 If none match after 10 clicks — close it out and move on, don't waste time
 
 Cross-check Redfin/Zillow listing simultaneously while clicking
 
 If listing shows GIS parcel map — use that to confirm location directly
 
 Four identical rectangular parcels side by side = process of elimination
 
 Use listing photos to confirm which specific one is correct

XVII. MAP LAYER TOGGLING PROTOCOL
★ Always toggle through multiple map layers on every property. Different satellite dates reveal different
information. One layer may show recent timbering, a new house, or a demolished structure that another
layer doesn't show.
Why Toggling Matters — Real Examples
Recent Timbering Caught
One layer shows mature canopy. New layer shows bare earth. Without toggling you'd think the timber is intact.
House Found/Not Found
One layer shows a structure. Older layer shows vacant land. Ask seller — 'Is there a house on the property?'
confirms current state.
House Demolished
Older layer shows a house. New layer shows it's gone. Seller may mention: 'Yeah, we tore it down two years
ago.'
Pond/Water Feature Missed
One satellite date captured dry season. Another captures wet season and reveals a pond or seasonal wetland.
Cleared vs. Wooded Discrepancy
Different dates show different clearing stages. Always compare old vs. new to understand trajectory.
Map Layer Toggle Steps
Click 'Map Layers' button (bottom right of screen, next to Data Layers). This gives access to different satellite
imagery dates. Toggle through available layers. Note any discrepancies. Flag everything unusual for the lead
manager. Old satellite may still show trees that have since been harvested. New satellite may show structures
or clearings that didn't exist before.

XVIII. FINAL PRE-DELIVERY SANITY CHECK
★ Run this check on every property before delivering the final analysis.
✓ Satellite vs. Analysis Match
Does the market value make sense against what the satellite shows? If it's heavily wooded, deeply rural, and
full of wetlands, a high MV doesn't make sense. If it's semi-rural cleared pasture near development, a low MV
doesn't make sense.
✓ PPA Curve Sanity
Does the MV align with the PPA trend I observed? If all comps are $4-6k ppa and my MV implies $9k ppa,
something is wrong. Recheck comp selection.
✓ Offer Range at ~50%
Is the offer range landing at approximately 50% of market value? That's the target. If not, recalculate.
✓ Nothing Missed on Satellite
Did I account for: easements, structures, wetlands, flood zone, recent timbering, water features, road access
issues, shape problems? Scroll back through satellite one final time.
✓ Hazards Layer Back ON
Are all hazard layers restored to ON? Never deliver with hazards toggled off.
✓ All MCP Tabs Closed
Close every tab opened during this comp. Delete the entire tab group. Browser will lag and crash if tabs stay
open.
✓ Complete 8-Section Response
Deliver everything in ONE response: Property Overview, Land Type, Area Assessment, Street View, Comp
Analysis, PPA Curve, Market Value, Offer Range. No breaks, no 'continue' requests.
