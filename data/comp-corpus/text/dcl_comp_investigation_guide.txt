INSTANT DISQUALIFIER #1 — OFF MARKET
■ OFF MARKET = INSTANT DISQUALIFY — DO NOT ANALYZE FURTHER
OFF MARKET means the property sold WITHOUT MLS exposure — no public listing, no competing buyer
pool, no open market price discovery. The price means nothing for comp purposes. It could be $1k/acre in a
$6k/acre market or $20k/acre in a $5k/acre market — neither tells you what a real buyer in the open market
would pay. STOP the moment you see it.

● Do not read the description. Do not check acreage. Do not pull APN. Zero additional time.
INSTANT DISQUALIFIER #2 — RELATED PARTY TRANSACTIONS
■ SAME AGENT/BROKERAGE ON BOTH SIDES = RELATED PARTY FLAG
When the listing agent and buying agent share the same last name or same brokerage — especially on a
parcel within a development the agent is actively selling — treat the sale price as potentially soft. The agent
may have sold to a family member at a preferred price, or bought it themselves to control inventory. Flag it
and weight it lower.
How to spot it: In the Zillow or Redfin listing details, scroll to the bottom of the description section. Look for
'Listed by: [Agent Name]' and 'Bought with: [Agent Name]'. If those two names share a last name or brokerage
name, flag it immediately.

INSTANT DISQUALIFIER #3 — QUIT CLAIM DEED
■ QUIT CLAIM DEED = NOT A MARKET TRANSACTION
A Quit Claim Deed is a legal instrument used for non-arms-length transfers: family transfers, estate
settlements, divorce proceedings, trust changes. The price recorded (often $0 or a nominal amount) has no
relationship to market value. When Land Insights shows 'Quit Claim Deed' as the deed type on a parcel's last
purchase, ignore that price completely.
● Quit Claim Deeds frequently show $0 sale price — this is a family/trust transfer, not a sale
● Even if a dollar amount is listed, it's often a nominal figure, not market value
● You'll see this in Land Insights under 'Last Purchase Type' and 'Deed Type' in the Summary panel
● Do not use any Quit Claim price as a comp — it contaminates your analysis

PART 2 — PHOTO ANALYSIS PROTOCOL
Photos tell you what descriptions are designed to hide
Listing descriptions are marketing documents. They are written to present the property in the most favorable
light. Every negative feature gets either omitted entirely or reframed as a positive. Photos are neutral — they
show exactly what is there without editorial spin. Always look at photos before trusting descriptions.
UTILITY POLES & POWER LINES IN PHOTOS
 14124 Healy Lake Rd, Bear Lake MI — street-level photo of the property. The utility pole is clearly visible in the middle distance
 running through the pine tree access path. This was NOT mentioned in the listing description.
The listing description for this property said: "Partially wooded 9.82 acre building site with electric already in."
Notice how the utility pole is reframed as 'electric already in' — a positive. The description is technically
accurate but completely changes how you read it. The photo tells the unvarnished truth.
✓ ALWAYS LOOK AT PHOTOS BEFORE READING THE DESCRIPTION
PHOTO FIRST RULE: Before reading a single word of the description, scroll through every available photo
looking specifically for: utility poles, overhead wires, cleared easement corridors, gates or fences indicating
access restrictions, nearby structures on adjacent parcels, and water features.

PART 4 — APN LOOKUP WORKFLOW
Step-by-step with screenshots — exact process, format issues, and failure protocol
The APN (Assessor's Parcel Number) lookup in Land Insights is the most reliable way to confirm which parcel a
comp represents. When you have an APN from Redfin or Zillow and need to verify it in Land Insights, follow this
exact sequence.
STEP 1 — Pull APN from listing details on Redfin or Zillow
In Redfin: scroll to 'Property Details' → 'Public Facts' section → 'APN' field. In Zillow: scroll to 'Facts & Features'
→ 'Details' section → 'Parcel number.' Copy the number exactly as shown — do not retype it.
 Redfin property details showing APN: 1303142505 for the Bear Creek parcel. This is the number you copy to use in Land Insights.
STEP 2 — Open a DUPLICATE Land Insights tab
Right-click the Land Insights tab → Duplicate. The new tab will open with the same view as your current
session. This preserves your working tab with the parcel panel open while you search in the new one.
The duplicated Land Insights tab opens in a fresh state — showing whatever parcel you were last viewing. The left panel shows the
 parcel data from the previous view. The search bar at top is where you will now work.

 Clicking the "Location" dropdown reveals three options: Location, Parcel #, Owner. Click "Parcel #" to switch into APN search

THE FORMAT MISMATCH PROBLEM — MOST COMMON APN FAILURE
CAUSE:
■ FORMAT MISMATCH IS THE #1 CAUSE OF 'NO RESULTS FOUND'
The same parcel number can be formatted completely differently across platforms. Redfin and Zillow often
strip all dashes and display a continuous digit string. Land Insights uses the county recorder's format which
includes dashes. Before giving up on an APN search, try reformatting the number.

STEP 6 — If 'No Results Found' persists — document and move on
If you've tried the original APN, tried reformatting with dashes in different positions, and still get nothing — the

'APN unverified — parcel not found in Land Insights.' Use the Redfin/Zillow map to approximate location
instead.

PART 6 — DEVELOPMENT COMP CONTEXT
The Chief Rd 289-acre subdivision — reading plot maps, developer pricing, related party
flags
When multiple similarly-sized parcels cluster in the same area along the same road, you're almost always
looking at a larger parent parcel being subdivided and sold off in lots. This completely changes how you use
these comps — the prices are set by a developer, not discovered through open-market competition between
multiple sellers.

 10.02ac | 6.39k (pending/pink), 9.99ac | 7.11k, 9.98ac | 8.92k (cyan). The repeated ~10-acre sizes and same road location =
 subdivision development.

parcel with matching owner, APN, and acreage. The other will show an adjacent parcel that happened to be
close to the GPS coordinates in the MLS record. Use the one that matches the Redfin/Zillow data.

PART 8 — THE COMPLETE COMP WORKFLOW
Every step in sequence from first click to final PPA decision
This is the master workflow that combines every technique in this guide. Follow it in sequence for every comp
you evaluate. Fast comps clear in 2 minutes. Complex comps like Bear Creek may take 15-20 minutes — but
you'll know everything there is to know about them.
STEP 1 — OPEN COMP IN LAND INSIGHTS — INSTANT STATUS CHECK
Click the comp label on the map. The popup shows: acreage, price, PPA, status, days on market. Check status
first. SOLD (MLS) = proceed. PENDING = proceed with discount note. OFF MARKET = close immediately.
PUBLIC RECORDS only = approach with extreme caution.
● Green label = sold MLS — proceed
● Pink label = pending — proceed, note 5-10% discount
● Cyan/blue label = active listing — proceed, note 10-15% ceiling discount
● No status or 'off market' = disqualify and close
STEP 2 — OPEN FULL COMP REPORT — PHOTO ANALYSIS FIRST
Click 'Details' or the comp title to open the full Land Insights comp report. LOOK AT PHOTOS BEFORE
READING ANYTHING ELSE. Count the photos. Note what you see.
● Utility poles, power lines, overhead wires → flag, check description
● Gate at entrance → access restriction, seasonal-only risk
● Creek, pond, river → begin waterfront premium investigation
● Shed, platform, campsite structures → confirms access, adds value
● Trail cam wildlife photos → hunting property buyer type
● Drone photos only → go find Street View for ground truth
● 'No Photo Available' on most slots → check Redfin AND Zillow separately
STEP 3 — READ THE DESCRIPTION — LOOK FOR WHAT'S MISSING
Read the full description. Compare what you saw in photos to what the description says. Note every omission
and every reframing.
● 'Electric already in' but utility pole visible in photo → noted
● 'Private setting' but neighbors visible in photo → noted
● 'Part of a 289-acre development' → development comp context applies
● 'Two rivers' → major waterfront premium, begin creek investigation
● No mention of access → potential landlocked, investigate

DewClaw Comp Investigation Guide | Page 28
● Description very short or 'No description available' → rely on photos and parcel data
STEP 4 — EXTRACT KEY DATA — ACREAGE, PRICE, APN, DOM
Record: exact acreage from listing, total price, calculated PPA, days on market, APN from Facts & Features.
Note any discrepancy between listing acreage and description acreage.
● Listing acreage ≠ description acreage → begin acreage investigation
● Very few days on market (under 30) → may have been underpriced
● Very many days on market (over 180) → may have been overpriced or had issues
● APN in standard county format → copy for Land Insights lookup
● APN in continuous digit string → copy, will need reformatting
STEP 5 — RUN APN LOOKUP IN LAND INSIGHTS
Duplicate Land Insights tab → Parcel # → County → Enter APN → Wait 3-5 seconds.
● Result found → confirm owner name, acreage, parcel matches
● 'No results found' → try reformatting (add dashes per county format)
● Still nothing → document as unverified, use Zillow lot-line map for location
● Wrong parcel appears (different owner) → duplicate pin problem, click other pin
● Correct parcel found → note adjacent owners, identify state/public land
STEP 6 — WATERFRONT INVESTIGATION (if creek/water present)
Any comp with water feature — creek, river, pond, lake frontage — requires this sub-sequence.
● Find the creek on the aerial → trace to nearest road crossing
● Open Google Maps → confirm creek name and road intersection
● Open Google Street View at the bridge → assess water quality visually
● Full flowing clear stream = year-round, premium → justify higher PPA
● Muddy, slow, seasonal-looking = discount → question the water premium claim
● Read property details for: frontage length, water body name, waterfront type
● Check for Blue Ribbon designation in description or county fishing resources
STEP 7 — LANDLOCKED INVESTIGATION (if no obvious road frontage)
If a parcel sells at a significant premium but shows no clear road frontage, investigate access.
● Check photos for shed, platform, campsite → built infrastructure = access exists
● Check photos for trail cams, hunting stands → water/foot access common in hunting properties
● Look for legal language in description: 'deeded easement', 'access via easement'
● Try to trace a physical path from road to parcel on the aerial

● If nothing found → note as seller question: 'How do you currently access this property?'
● A $10k+ ppa landlocked parcel almost certainly has legal easement OR water access
STEP 8 — DEVELOPMENT CONTEXT CHECK
If multiple similarly-sized parcels cluster along same road within small area:
● Check listing description for: 'part of a [X]-acre parcel', 'additional parcels available'
● Look for plot maps in listing photos — lettered/numbered lots = formal subdivision
● Note which lots sold vs pending vs active within development
● Identify floor comp (lowest ppa in development) and ceiling comp (highest)
● Flag related party buyers: same last name as listing agent = possible insider price
● Weight development comps lower than standalone arm's-length sales
● Use floor comp as your baseline; development ceiling may not reflect true open market
STEP 9 — FINAL PPA DETERMINATION
After running all applicable investigations, make your final comp call.
● Straight sale, verified, no issues → use PPA as stated
● Waterfront premium → note premium and isolate from non-waterfront comps
● Acreage discrepancy → use midpoint PPA between both acreage scenarios
● Development context → weight lower, use as reference not primary comp
● Related party flag → weight lower, possible soft price
● Long DOM (90+ days) → may have been overpriced, discount slightly
● Short DOM (under 14 days) → may have been underpriced, note as floor
● Document your reasoning → your comp notes must explain every weight adjustment
